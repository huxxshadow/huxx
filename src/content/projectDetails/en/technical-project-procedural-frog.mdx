## My Role

A personal project: a playable, cartoonishly exaggerated frog, built from scratch in Unity:

* **Body**: four legs from key-pose blending and analytic IK, a trunk that stretches, pitches, yaws and rolls; general rules in the pose keep the joints out of the ground.
* **Hopping**: the arc solved back from the landing point, the launch angle adapted to the drop and the arc sampled for clearance; landings caught by springs that carry the momentum.
* **Crawling and turning**: a lateral-sequence gait, footholds found by raycasts, the trunk fitted to the ground; turning on the spot with the same steps, not by hopping.
* **Tongue and props**: a Verlet-rope tongue with predictive aim that catches a fly; a coin that is a real rigid body, swallowed and spat out again.
* **Visual layer and verification**: squash and stretch, belly, throat, blinking and eyes pressed in, muscles bulging; an intent-based controller (click where to hop); deterministic replay, frame-by-frame baselines and numeric probes.

## Project Details

The timing comes from real frogs: the jump is a catapult — crouching loads the tendons, then the hips, knees and ankles straighten in turn all at once; in the air the hind legs trail and the front legs reach forward; it lands on its front legs, the body pitching down and the eyes pulled into the head; the tongue flicks out, sticks and comes back in an instant. On top of that, cartoon exaggeration: deeper squash, bouncier recoil, muscles that visibly take the load.

### Body: Key Poses and Analytic IK

* **Poses as data**: a key pose is the trunk plus an end target for each leg (position, knee or elbow direction, foot direction), mirrored left to right; poses blend directly, and each leg can also take its own foothold and ground height.
* **Legs**: two segments in front, three behind, all analytic IK, drawn back toward the hip when out of reach.
* **Rules in the pose**: a knee or elbow about to go into the ground turns round the circle about the hip–ankle line to the nearest point clear of it, continuously; a shoulder or hip in the ground lifts the whole trunk; a planted hand out of reach pulls the trunk back toward it.
* **Which way the knee points**: when turning, any fixed bending direction will at some point line up with the leg and flip, so the knee starts from where it was last frame and turns only a small step each frame — always continuous.

### Hopping: The Arc Solved Back, Momentum on Landing

* **Crouch and push**: crouch, hold, burst; the trunk follows a Hermite curve up to the take-off velocity, the hind feet pinned without sliding.
* **In the air**: the trunk follows an analytic parabola, its take-off velocity solved from the landing point so the front paws land right on the target; the higher the target the steeper the launch, and the ground is sampled along the arc before take-off, steepening it further where there is no room.
* **Landing**: the front paws pin and the trunk hangs on a spring to the pose, carrying its momentum past the hands, sinking until its head presses the ground, then springing back up to sit; pitch has a spring of its own that the surge keeps tipping forward. The hind legs tuck back under it along a Bézier arc.

$$
\begin{gathered}
T = \sqrt{\frac{2\,(d\tan\theta - \Delta y)}{g}} \\[6pt]
\mathbf v_h = \frac{\mathbf d}{T} \\[6pt]
v_y = \frac{\Delta y + \tfrac12 g T^2}{T} \\[6pt]
\ddot{\mathbf x} = \omega^2\left(\mathbf x_{\text{pose}} - \mathbf x\right) - 2\zeta\omega\,\dot{\mathbf x}
\end{gathered}
$$

The first three solve the launch back from the landing point: $\mathbf d$ the displacement over the ground (of length $d$), $\Delta y$ the difference in height, $\theta$ the launch angle, $g$ gravity, $T$ the flight time. The last is the spring the trunk hangs on to its pose as it lands: $\omega$ the natural frequency, $\zeta$ the damping ratio, underdamped so it overshoots and springs back.

### Crawling and Turning on the Spot

* **Gait**: the frog's lateral-sequence gait (left hind → left front → right hind → right front). Each foot steps to where the body will be when the step ends, raycast down to the real ground; feet lift quickly and set down gently.
* **Trunk**: its height and tilt fitted to the ground under the four feet; its speed pulses with each push of the hind legs, and its weight shifts onto the planted side while a foot is up.
* **Turning**: well off its goal, it steps round on the spot first, then walks; stopping, it takes a few more steps until all four feet are exactly back in the sitting pose. The feet never slide.

### Tongue and Coin

* **Tongue**: a Verlet rope under gravity, its root fixed at the front of the jaw; the tip's mass includes whatever is stuck to it, so something heavy bows the rope. Aiming, the mouth opens and the head turns; it shoots at where the prey will be when the tongue arrives.
* **Collision**: the tongue stays above the ground and outside the coin, pushed again after the coin moves, so nothing passes through.
* **Coin**: a rigid disc with mass and an inertia tensor; on the tongue it is held by a soft position constraint (PBD), so it swings and spins, its stuck face always turned toward the tongue. On the ground: impulse contacts (restitution and friction) with rolling resistance, sleeping once still.
* **Swallowing**: swallowing a fly, it presses its eyes down into its head to push the food; with the coin in its mouth the throat swells into a big lump, then the head tips back and flicks forward to spit it out.

$$
\begin{gathered}
\mathbf x_i \leftarrow \mathbf x_i + (1 - c)\left(\mathbf x_i - \mathbf x_i^{\text{prev}}\right) + \mathbf g\,h^2 \\[6pt]
\Delta\mathbf x_i = \frac{w_i}{w_i + w_{i+1}}\left(\lVert\mathbf r\rVert - \ell\right)\hat{\mathbf r} \\[6pt]
\Delta\mathbf x_{i+1} = -\frac{w_{i+1}}{w_i + w_{i+1}}\left(\lVert\mathbf r\rVert - \ell\right)\hat{\mathbf r} \\[6pt]
\mathbf r = \mathbf x_{i+1} - \mathbf x_i
\end{gathered}
$$

The first is Verlet integration with a damping $c$, the rest the length constraint between neighbouring points ($\ell$ a segment's length). $w$ is the inverse mass: 0 at the root (fixed to the jaw), smaller at the tip with something stuck to it, so heavy prey bows the rope.

### Visual Layer

The visual state all advances in the simulation step and the skin only reads it, so every frame is deterministic.

* **Squash and stretch**: stretched when fast, squashed by the impact on landing, an underdamped spring letting it wobble.
* **Flesh**: the belly sags and sways with the trunk's acceleration; the harder the front legs are loaded, the more the arms bulge and the elbows push out; when the trunk stops short, the flesh on the back carries on and springs back.
* **Face**: a pumping throat, blinks, eyes pulled in and squinting on landing, eyes on the landing point while crouching.
