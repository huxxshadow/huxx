## My Role

As project lead and data scientist, I was responsible for the complete pipeline from business problem definition to model analysis and delivery. My main responsibilities included:

- **End-to-End Machine Learning Solution Design and Execution**: Designed and led the full analytical framework around the core problem of "online shopper purchase intention prediction," covering data understanding, feature engineering, model evaluation, and business translation.
- **Data Processing and Feature Engineering**: Completed raw data cleaning, variable encoding, skewed distribution handling, and feature construction, engineering derived features such as `TotalPages`, `TotalDuration`, `AvgProductDuration`, `ProductPageRatio`, and `Bounce_Exit_Ratio`, and validated their actual contribution to model performance.
- **Classification Modeling, Hyperparameter Tuning, and Class Imbalance Handling**: Ran stratified cross-validation across Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM; systematically compared SMOTE, `class_weight`, and `scale_pos_weight` strategies for handling class imbalance; and tuned core tree models using `RandomizedSearchCV`.
- **Model Evaluation and Decision Threshold Optimization**: Used F1-score, ROC-AUC, Precision, and Recall as core metrics, optimized classification thresholds using Precision-Recall Curves, and applied cost-sensitive analysis to link model outputs to business decisions such as coupon distribution and customer conversion value.
- **Model Interpretability and User Segmentation Analysis**: Used SHAP, Permutation Importance, and PDP to explain key feature effects; combined K-Means, Elbow Method, Silhouette Score, and PCA to perform user clustering and persona analysis, providing clearer data support for targeted marketing and operational segmentation.
- **Results Reporting and Interactive Presentation**: Integrated the analysis pipeline, model results, and business recommendations into a presentable demo and reproducible analysis output for external presentation and internal team communication.

## Project Details

In this project, I built a relatively complete purchase intention prediction pipeline from scratch. During the early stage, I conducted EDA focused on data distribution, class imbalance, variable correlations, and user behavioral differences, followed by feature engineering and encoding. In the modeling phase, I systematically compared multiple classifiers including Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM, using stratified cross-validation to ensure stable and reliable results. To address the class imbalance of approximately **5.46:1** between positive and negative samples, I compared **SMOTE, class_weight, and scale_pos_weight** strategies, and through tuning and threshold optimization, identified **XGBoost (Tuned)** as the overall best-performing model, achieving **F1 = 0.6750 and ROC-AUC = 0.9235** on the test set.

Beyond model performance, I placed particular emphasis on interpretability and business applicability. On the interpretability side, I used **SHAP, Permutation Importance, and PDP** to decompose the model and identified **PageValues, ExitRates, BounceRates, and Month_Nov** as the most influential features for purchase intention. On the business side, I combined model predictions with value scenarios to complete cost-sensitive analysis across different thresholds, finding that if the objective is to maximize net commercial benefit, the decision threshold can be further adjusted to **0.3**. Additionally, I used **K-Means clustering** to segment users into **3 typical groups** — high-activity browsers, general visitors, and low-engagement bouncers — providing clearer data-driven support for targeted marketing, page optimization, and tiered operations.
