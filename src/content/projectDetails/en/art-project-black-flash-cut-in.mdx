## My Role

A solo demo in Unreal Engine 5. The characters and animations are Epic's Paragon assets; every system, shader and line of code on top of them is mine:

* **Presentation System**: A presentation director that layers full-screen effects over gameplay, timed in real seconds so the slow motion it causes never slows the effect itself.
* **Post-Process Shaders**: Two post-process materials written as HLSL — the Black Flash impact frame and the Persona-style skill cut-in — both built from the real rendered image rather than overlaid sprites or particles.
* **Camera Language**: A cut-in camera that solves its own framing, avoids occluders, and follows a charge-then-release rhythm.
* **Combat Feel and Designer Tooling**: Hit stop, directional hit reactions on a dummy that only reacts and never moves on its own, every timing exposed as an editable curve or value, and debug keys to fire each effect instantly.

## Project Details

The goal was a small, sharp portfolio piece for technical art and combat design: not a full fighting game, but one fight pushed as far as it will go on presentation. Two references set the bar — the **Black Flash** impact frames from *Jujutsu Kaisen*, and the skill cut-ins of *Persona 5* and *Persona 3 Reload*.

### Black Flash: Post-Processing the Real Frame

The first version drew its own effect — lightning, a shockwave disc, particles — and it read as a pasted-on decal. The rule that replaced it: **don't draw an effect, re-draw the frame.** The Black Flash is a single full-screen post-process that reads the scene color, depth and custom depth of the actual frame and turns it into black-and-white drawings.

* **Four drawings, hard cuts**: In 0.2 seconds the screen cuts through four distinct "key drawings", each held for one beat, the way an animator would time an impact frame. The world freezes for exactly the same 0.2 seconds so all four drawings sit on the same moment.
* **Characters stay readable**: The attacker and the victim are switched to custom depth for the flash, so every drawing knows exactly where the characters are. They get a sharp threshold and edge lines — never blur — while the background is free to smear, invert or dissolve.
* **Speed lines from the hit point**: The impact location is projected to screen space, and thin, crisp speed lines radiate from it, inverted against the base so they read on both black and white.
* **Works in any lighting**: Thresholds are relative to the frame's average brightness, so the same drawing holds up in a bright sky shot or a dark close-up.
* **Composition per drawing**: Every drawing gets its own slight zoom and shake, like the framing shifting between hand-drawn frames.

### Skill Cut-In: Persona-Style Close-Up

Casting a skill hard-cuts to a telephoto close-up of the caster while the world drops to 5% speed. The caster alone writes custom depth, so the shader can cut them out, flatten them into three tone steps with ink outlines, and lay a diagonal color band behind them: red with halftone dots for the Persona 5 style, blue with speed stripes for Persona 3. Only the skill name appears, riding on the band.

### Rhythm: Charge, Then Release

A cut-in that only slides in and out feels flat. The timing was reshaped into a beat structure:

1. **Cut in**: the band slams in with a slight overshoot and the camera makes a quick first push.
2. **Charge**: the camera creeps closer while a tremble builds; the name pulls back a little, like a drawn bow.
3. **Release**: the camera snaps in, the tremble stops, a light flash hits, and the band and name fly off.

Every part of that — band motion, name motion, camera push and camera tremble — is a curve on the skill's data, editable in the Details panel before pressing Play, so each skill can have its own timing.
