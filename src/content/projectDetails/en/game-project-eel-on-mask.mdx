## My Role

In this team project developed over 48 hours for Global Game Jam 2026 Hong Kong, I served as team leader, lead programmer, and game designer. My main responsibilities covered overall development workflow organization, core mechanic implementation, and visual cohesion. Specific responsibilities included:

* **Team Collaboration and Pipeline Setup**: As team leader, I led a 9-person team through project scoping, task breakdown, version integration, and rapid iteration, establishing a collaboration workflow suited to the high-pressure pace of a Game Jam to allow gameplay, art, programming, and audio to proceed in parallel.
* **Core Gameplay and Level Logic Design**: Designed the game's mechanics around the central concept of "Clipping Mask," enabling players to change sticker materials to affect scene rules, traversal methods, and puzzle logic, constructing a level experience that combines platforming and puzzle-solving.
* **Custom Grid and TileMap System Development**: Built a grid system supporting custom physics rules and extended it with a configurable-rule TileMap solution, enabling different material zones, trap tiles, and special interactions to be driven by a unified system and iterated on quickly.
* **Shader and Visual Polish**: Implemented an outline sticker effect and CRT post-processing using Unity 2D Shaders, unified and enhanced the project's overall art style, and improved visual distinctiveness and presentation quality within the limited development time.

## Project Details

*Eel On Mask（鳗！）* is a 2D puzzle platformer developed in **48 hours** for **Global Game Jam 2026 Hong Kong**. Players take on the role of Eel, using the mysterious **"Clipping Mask"** ability to change sticker materials and travel between worlds, solve puzzles, and gradually uncover the truth behind the story. The project was later tidied up and officially released on Steam as a free indie title.

### Team Organization and 48-Hour Rapid Development

Because the project was born from a Game Jam, development time was extremely limited — team collaboration efficiency was itself the key to whether the project could succeed. As team leader, I quickly locked in the core creative direction at the project's outset and organized the team around the goal of "achievable in a short time while still being distinctive," completing gameplay scoping, task breakdown, and priority ordering.

Through an AI-assisted collaboration pipeline, combined with per-directory README conventions and GitHub version constraints, we unified development boundaries, asset structure, and the merge process, enabling efficient parallel development within a short cycle.

### Clipping Mask: Translating a Graphic Concept into Game Mechanics

The core gameplay turns the Clipping Mask from image editing into a material-driven puzzle mechanic. An object's material decides not only how it looks but also rule properties such as collision, load-bearing and environmental feedback; players rebuild paths and solve spatial puzzles by switching materials.

Levels teach progressively: they introduce a single rule first, then layer in objects, materials and spatial relationships, expanding strategic depth while keeping the cost of understanding under control.

### Custom Grid System and Rule-Driven TileMap

To support this "variable rules" gameplay, I implemented a **grid system with custom physics logic** and built on top of it a **TileMap system** supporting special rule extensions. Compared to simply relying on static colliders, this approach better suited the project, since the same area needed to exhibit different interactive behavior under different states.

By abstracting level areas into a unified grid data structure, I could more efficiently handle traversability, collision logic, and trap responses under different material states. At the code level, rather than hardcoding each special case, behaviors for different tiles are driven by rule configuration. This design improved system extensibility on one hand and significantly increased level building efficiency on the other, making it particularly well-suited to the high-frequency trial-and-error and rapid iteration of a Game Jam environment.

In practice, this system helped us complete scene interactions with stable logic in a very short time, and also left room for adding more material effects and special level mechanics afterward.

### Using Shaders to Unify the Sticker World's Visual Language

On the visual side, I focused on unifying the project's overall style. Because Game Jam teams work under short timelines with varied asset sources and production methods, without a unifying pass the final visuals can easily feel stylistically fragmented. I therefore applied Unity 2D Shaders to reprocess core visual elements, bringing them more in line with the project's "sticker world" setting.

First is the **outline sticker Shader**. This effect reinforces the edge definition and layering of game objects, making it easier for players to visually distinguish interactable objects from environmental backgrounds, while also strengthening the thematic sense of "stickers being cut, layered, and replaced." Second, I added a **CRT post-processing effect** — through subtle scanlines, color shifts, and screen texture, adding a unified visual atmosphere to the whole work.
