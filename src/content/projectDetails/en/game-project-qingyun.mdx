*Qingyun* is a third-person action boss fight made in Unreal Engine 5.

## My Role

This is a solo project. I built the following in Unreal Engine 5 with C++, Niagara, materials and Python editor scripts:

* **Boss rework and stone system**: Paragon's Rampage turned into a rock colossus of procedural floating stones, with the stones carried through all three phases: they form the body, get knocked off, are drawn out as rune stones, drive into the ground, and finally all rise into a vortex.
* **Three-phase boss AI**: move selection, disabled moves and a faster tempo after an arm is lost, twin pillars with their own AI, and a rune storm fired on a beat.
* **Combat feel**: a light combo, a tiered charged heavy attack, a perfect-dodge time stop, part-based lock-on, hit stop and camera impact; every feel parameter is exposed in Project Settings and tunable at runtime.
* **Realistic lightning VFX**: procedurally generated fractal lightning meshes plus Niagara particles, covering the charge, the impact discharge, telegraphs and shattering stones.
* **Leaves and wind**: thousands of interactive instanced leaves on a procedural wind field, with debug views and an offline lab.
* **Environment and lighting**: the bamboo clearing built in layers, lit by Ultra Dynamic Sky.
* **Cameras and staging**: skill cut-ins, execution cameras and the final execution.
* **Tools**: a recording director, a cover-shot script, offline tuning tools, and the project's trailer and devlog.

## Project Details

### From a Paragon Asset to a Rock Colossus

The boss starts from Paragon's stock Rampage (Elemental skin). Rather than remodelling it, I reworked it layer by layer:

<figure>
    <video class="clip" src={bossBuildClip} poster={bossBuildClipPoster} width="1280" height="550" muted loop playsinline preload="none" data-autoplay aria-label="The boss rework"></video>
    <figcaption>The layers added one by one from the same camera: materials, floating stones, halo, tail fan, shoulder pillars, chains</figcaption>
</figure>

* **Materials**: the orange lava cracks became jade energy cracks, and the original shoulder rune was removed.
* **Rocks cut out and replaced by procedural stones**: an editor tool cuts the rocks on the mesh into separate static meshes by connected region, hides those parts of the original mesh, and replaces them with procedural floating stones. Each stone follows its bone on a damped spring, with bobbing, drift and hit jolts, and carries rune decals.
* **Halo, tail fan and shoulder pillars**: all built from the same stone system; the tail fan flies out in the intro and stands around the arena as a barrier.
* **Chains**: strings of stones swimming around the body, the ammunition for the later rune stones.
* **Chaos destruction**: geometry collections are pre-generated for the rocks, the forearms and every region of the body, used for knock-offs, arm breaks and the final shatter.

The cut is a one-off editor tool: over Rampage's rock material slots it runs a union-find on shared vertices to separate every connected rock, re-orients each piece along its principal axes, saves it as its own static mesh, and records which bone it hangs from, its offset and its rest rotation. It produces 29 rocks, and those records give every floating stone its target at runtime. The halo uses 29 stones; the chains are 11 strings with 112 stones in all.

### The Stones' Journey Through Three Phases

The stone system is designed so that the same stones keep changing roles over the whole fight, and everything the player knocks off comes back.

        <figcaption>Intro: stones fly up off the ground and assemble the boss</figcaption>
    </figure>
    <figure>
        <video class="clip" src={hitZonesClip} poster={hitZonesClipPoster} width="1280" height="550" muted loop playsinline preload="none" data-autoplay aria-label="Hit zone visualisation"></video>
        <figcaption>Hit zones: white is the body, orange the armoured arms</figcaption>
    </figure>
</div>

* **Phase one**: sweep, slam and leap slam. The hero's attacks knock small stones off the boss, and the pieces stay on the ground. The arms are armoured and hits are resolved per body part.
* **Arm breaks**: an arm can be executed once it reaches its health threshold; its stones fall away and the forearm shatters. Losing one arm starts phase two: moves that need that hand are disabled and the whole move set speeds up.
* **Twin pillars**: the two pillars on his back tear free and roam above the centre of the arena with their own AI, attacking alongside the boss; only a fully charged heavy attack can shatter them.
* **Rune storm**: chain stones are drawn out into a ring above the boss and fired at the hero on a beat, each on its own flight path and with escort stones. Swinging on the beat breaks them one by one; stones that get through drive into the ground whole, return to the chains when the storm ends, and leave copies standing in the ground. The last stone can be batted back into the boss with a heavy attack.
* **Vortex**: once both arms are gone, every broken piece on the ground, the planted copies and the stones at the arena's edge rise into a vortex around the arena.

<div class="image-grid-2">
    <Image src={imgDebugHitZones} alt="Hit zone debug view: white is the body, orange the armoured arms; the boss's live state at the top right" />
    <Image src={imgDebugAttackShape} alt="Attack debug view: the slam's hit volume (red wireframe)" />
</div>

* **Hit zones**: every body part is a set of spheres that follow the bones, and hits are resolved per part; the armoured arms deflect light attacks, so only heavy attacks and executions deal damage. The debug view shows health, break gauge, the state of both arms and the remaining stones live at the top right.
* **Attack volumes**: each move's hit volume switches on and off along its animation time, and the debug view draws the active one as a wireframe (the slam, right).
* **Health gates**: each arm has a health threshold; once it is reached, health locks until that arm's execution is done. The last stretch of health locks too, so only the final execution can end the fight and the three phases always come in order.

* **The storm's beat**: firing runs on real time, rising from 3 to 9.5 stones a second; each volley grows from 1 to 8 stones and flight times shorten as it goes. Every stone flies a quadratic Bézier curve whose control point is pushed randomly to the sides and up or down, so each path differs while all of them end where the hero's swing passes.
* **Staff meets stone**: when attack is pressed, the next stone about to arrive is locked; the swing's animation speed is set from that stone's remaining flight time and the stone's pace is nudged, so the staff visibly connects with it on screen rather than registering a hit at a distance.

### Combat Feel

Everything about how the hero feels is a parameter, gathered in Chinese-labelled Project Settings panels and live while playing: combo timing, charge tiers, hit reach, hit stop, camera shake, FOV punch and more.

<div class="image-grid-2">
    <figure>
        <video class="clip" src={perfectDodgeClip} poster={perfectDodgeClipPoster} width="1280" height="550" muted loop playsinline preload="none" data-autoplay aria-label="Perfect dodge"></video>
        <figcaption>Perfect dodge: dodging just before a hit stops time</figcaption>
    </figure>
    <figure>
        <video class="clip" src={lockOnClip} poster={lockOnClipPoster} width="1280" height="550" muted loop playsinline preload="none" data-autoplay aria-label="Part lock-on"></video>
        <figcaption>Lock-on switches between body parts (left arm / body / right arm)</figcaption>
    </figure>
</div>

* **Impact**: on a hit the world slows for a beat, the hero's swing holds at the point of contact, and the camera shakes along the blow with an FOV punch. The boss never slows the hero down; all of the feel lives on the hero's side.
* **Perfect dodge**: a curve-driven time stop, with the camera following the slow motion.
* **Lock-on**: targets are found from where the camera looks, and the mouse wheel steps between the left arm, the body and the right arm.

* **Hit stop values**: about 0.06 s of world slow for light hits and 0.11 s for heavy ones, with the recovery scaled to the stop's length; the hero's animation freezes briefly at the point of contact while the camera keeps following.

### Realistic Lightning

A fully charged heavy attack carries lightning. The first version used thick glowing arcs that looked too cartoonish; the final version uses thin fractal bolts that discharge with the rhythm of real lightning.

<figure>
    <video class="clip" src={lightningCompareClip} poster={lightningCompareClipPoster} width="1280" height="550" muted loop playsinline preload="none" data-autoplay aria-label="Lightning before and after"></video>
    <figcaption>Left: the early cartoon arcs. Right: the realistic fractal lightning now</figcaption>
</figure>

* **Charge**: current flows along the staff and gathers at its tip, where a surge fans back along the staff; at full charge it bursts outward once.
* **Impact discharge**: branching discharge channels shoot out from the point of impact, with leader, return stroke and re-strike in turn, then vanish quickly; the reach is up to half the screen.
* **Implementation**: the bolt meshes are generated procedurally every frame (a random walk with finer jitter on top), together with Niagara sparks, grit and dust.

* **Timing of a discharge**: one burst lasts about 0.6 s. The leader grows out of the impact point in about 0.035 s, the whole channel then flashes (the return stroke), and weaker re-strikes replay the same paths at about 0.11 s and 0.19 s; the channel shapes stay fixed throughout.
* **Geometry**: each channel has 22 segments from a direction-biased random walk with finer jitter on top; about 40% of the nodes along it sprout branches, some of which branch again. Every segment becomes a camera-facing strip with a thin white core and a soft glow, kept fine enough that it never smears into a blob at a distance.
* **Reuse**: the same discharge function drives the cyan burst when a rune stone is shattered (10 bolts, 0.2 s).

* **Interaction**: light attacks sample points along the staff and inject delayed gusts to sweep leaves; the charged heavy draws leaves in while charging and blasts them out on release; heavy hits also shake leaves down from the bamboo canopy; the skill drags a whirlwind along with the hero.
* **Wind field**: global wind (with breathing and gusts), local eddies, air pushed by characters and whirlwinds add up to one field; each leaf has its own drag and flip, and the leaves use a two-sided material that glows through when flipped.
* **Performance**: only disturbed instances are updated, distant leaves step less often, and every part is timed on the game thread against a 1 ms budget.

* **Lab workflow**: a recording stores per-frame leaves, wind field and events; replays use a fixed random seed and a fixed time step, so results repeat exactly; sweeping one parameter over several values produces side-by-side plots and a metrics table (leaves lifted, peak height, mean travel, landing time, cost and so on) to choose from.
* **Rendering and updates**: the carpet is split into small instanced meshes; only the instances moved this frame are submitted in a batch, together with their previous transforms so TSR gets correct motion vectors and the leaves don't smear; each instanced mesh has fixed bounds, so they are never recomputed per frame.
* **Lookup and tiers**: a spatial hash finds the resting leaves near a point; leaves in flight are stepped less often by distance in near, mid and far tiers, with far ones updated every other frame.

### Tools

* **Recording director**: runs a timetable of commands in the game to stage positions, switch parameters, trigger moves and move cameras; most of the devlog's shots were captured with it automatically.
* **Cover shots**: pauses on a chosen moment, places the camera for a given composition and writes high-resolution screenshots.
* **Test commands**: a set of console commands triggers any boss move, arm break or phase change and any hero action, for self-testing and recording.
* **Editor automation**: building the arena, generating materials and Niagara systems in batches and importing fonts are all Python editor scripts run in the editor through a local interface, so every change is repeatable and traceable.
