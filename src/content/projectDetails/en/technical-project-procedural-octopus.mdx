## My Role

A personal project: an octopus on land, built from scratch in Unity, all eight arms generated procedurally with no keyframes:

* **Arms**: each arm is a curvature field integrated segment by segment along its length, so it keeps its length and never kinks; a reach is a bend wave running from the root to the tip.
* **Locomotion**: terrain-sampled footholds and an arrhythmic haul gait, the body dragged by the arms holding on; a spiral pole climb planned on the unrolled cylinder, and a bar swing as a damped pendulum.
* **Wrapping and grasping**: helical wrap curves round poles, a soft column and props; the grip slides along the arm to thread a handle; held things have weight and sag on a spring.
* **Props**: Verlet cloth flags with per-facet wind; a soft column dented by filleted grooves, the displaced volume bulging out between the coils.
* **Collision and skin**: the arms projected out of the ground, props and body every frame; a layered chromatophore model drives the colour, quantized to the pixel palette; deterministic replay and numeric probes for verification.

## Project Details

The motion follows research on real octopuses: the near part of an arm mostly lengthens and shortens while the far part mostly bends; crawling has no fixed rhythm, the body pushed and pulled by attached arms lengthening and shortening; in a reach a bend point travels from the root to the tip and the arm straightens behind it. Out of the water there is no buoyancy, so the timing is slower, the body stays low and the mantle trails behind. Looks come first — clean, continuous motion — with physics only assisting.

### Arms: Curvature Fields

* **Shapes are written, not solved**: an arm is its root plus a curvature at each joint (turn per metre about the frame's three axes), integrated from root to tip, every segment a piece of arc. So the arm is always exactly as long as its segments, it never kinks, and its frames never twist on their own — the back stays up, the suckers down.
* **Motion adds up in curvature**: the rest pose + the tip's curl + a slow wave along the far part + exploring sweeps + the reach. Two shapes blend by blending their curvatures, never their positions.
* **Reaching**: the arm first coils over its back with its root turning to the target, then the bend point runs from the root to the tip, slow, fast, slow; the arm behind it straightens and thins.
* **Constant volume**: an arm thins as it is stretched and thickens as it shortens.

$$
\mathbf q_{j+1} = \mathbf q_j \exp\!\left(\tfrac{\ell}{2}\,\boldsymbol\kappa_j\right)

\mathbf x_{j+1} = \mathbf x_j + \ell\,\mathbf q_j\hat{\mathbf z}
$$

$$
r = \frac{r_0}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
$$

The first two integrate along the arm: $\mathbf q_j$ is segment $j$'s frame (a quaternion), $\boldsymbol\kappa_j$ the curvature vector at that joint, $\ell$ the segment length and $\hat{\mathbf z}$ the frame's tangent. The last is the constant-volume rule: $\lambda$ is the stretch and $r_0$ the rest radius.

### Crawling: A Haul Gait

* **Footholds**: each arm samples the terrain in its own sector round the body and projects its foothold onto the surface; the curve from root to foothold is a Hermite curve, its stretch capped so an arm is never pulled out like a rubber band.
* **Hauling**: the body does not slide along a path; it is dragged in pulses by the arms holding on — the arms tense and the body draws back a little, then it surges forward as they shorten.
* **No rhythm**: there is no fixed step rate; the most strained arm steps first, neighbouring arms never lift together, and several arms always hold the ground.
* **Body pose**: height and tilt follow the surface normal fitted to the footholds, so the whole body settles onto rocks and steps.

### Climbing: The Unrolled Cylinder

* **Unrolled mapping**: the pole's surface is unrolled into a plane in which an arm's angle round the mouth is its angle round the pole; footholds are planned in that plane and rolled back onto the pole.
* **Helical path**: the body climbs along a helix, and footholds are placed ahead by the body's angular speed round the pole — the front arms reach and grip above, the back arms brace below.
* **Wrap direction**: each arm wraps starting from the side it approaches from, so arms never cross behind the pole; the top of the pole is a limit no arm reaches past.
* **The bar**: four arms wind round the bar first, then the body leaves the platform and swings beneath it as a pendulum; the more arms hold, the stronger the damping, and the remaining arms take hold one by one as it settles.

$$
\theta_a = \theta_b + \Theta\tanh\!\left(\frac{k\,\varphi_a}{\Theta}\right) + \omega\,\tau
$$

$$
\ddot\phi = -\frac{g'}{L}\sin\phi - c(n)\,\dot\phi
$$

The first is the unrolled mapping: $\varphi_a$ is arm $a$'s angle round the mouth, $\theta_b$ the body's angle round the pole, $\Theta$ the furthest round an arm may reach, and $\omega\tau$ the lead from the angular speed $\omega$. The second is the damped pendulum on the bar: $\phi$ the swing angle, $L$ its length, $g'$ a gravity lightened for the look, and $c(n)$ growing with the number $n$ of arms on the bar.

### Wrapping and Grasping

* **Helical wraps**: past the grip the arm winds along a helix round the grip axis; the wrap grows from the contact toward the tip and unwinds back from the tip when letting go.
* **The soft column**: only the three nearest arms wrap, each starting on its own root's side and at its own height, tightening in steps; the column dents in filleted grooves, and the displaced volume bulges out between the coils.
* **A sliding grip**: the grip is not fixed to one segment but slides along the arm — to thread a handle, the thin part near the tip leads through first, then the arm winds tight.
* **Weight**: nothing is lifted until the arm has wound round it; once lifted, a thing hangs on a spring and sags and rebounds by its mass, and a heavier one makes the arm work harder.

### Cloth Flags

* **Verlet cloth**: a grid of particles with structural, shear and bend distance constraints relaxed iteratively, substeps adapted to the frame time so it never freezes in real time.
* **Wind**: each facet is pushed by the wind's speed relative to it along its normal, with turbulent gusts on top; the pole hangs from the arm, so waving it whips the cloth.
* **Many flags**: six arms each hold a flag and wave to a beat, every cloth solved on its own.

$$
\mathbf F = k\,A\,(\mathbf w\cdot\hat{\mathbf n})\,\lvert\mathbf w\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\rvert\,\hat{\mathbf n}

\mathbf w = \mathbf u_{\text{wind}} - \mathbf v
$$

$\hat{\mathbf n}$ is the facet's normal, $A$ its area, $\mathbf v$ its velocity, $\mathbf w$ the relative wind and $k$ the air's density times the drag coefficient.

### Collision and Skin

* **Collision projection**: last thing each frame, the arms' nodes are projected out of the ground, rocks and poles; held props get one more strict pass at the very end, so no arm goes through a prop. Arms keep their distance from each other and from the body by their drawn thickness.
* **Colour**: a layered chromatophore model — darkness, mottling, dark clouds passing over the body, papillae raised or smoothed — each eased on its own, then quantized to the pixel palette.
* **Sleep**: in quiet sleep it pales, closes its eyes and breathes slowly; in active sleep its patterns flash through quickly, papillae rise, and its arms and eyes twitch.
